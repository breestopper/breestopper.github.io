AMC Theatres app redesign

Simplifying ticket booking, making membership benefits visible, and bringing group planning into the app.

Role
UX researcher and designer. Ran two of the five usability sessions
Team
Three designers
Timeline
Fall 2025
Context
Introduction to UX Design, Pace University
Tools
Figma, POP by Marvel, Zoom
Note
Academic project by Pace University students, for educational purposes only. Not affiliated with AMC Theatres

The problem

People like going to AMC for the immersive screens and the ability to pick a seat. The app is where the experience gets harder. Our goal was to help people book faster, coordinate with friends more easily, and get more from the app than a ticket.

Research

We ran six semi-structured interviews of 45 to 50 minutes each, combining questions with observed tasks in the current app. Two participants used the app often and four used it occasionally. We grouped what we heard into an affinity diagram with five clusters and built two personas from it: Anna, who plans movie nights for a group, and Alex, an enthusiast who wants trailers, reviews, and favorites.

“We coordinate seats over chat… then one person books.”

Interview participant
  • Going to the movies is social. People pick seats over group chat, then one person books and collects money later.
  • Frequent users found the app efficient. Occasional users found it cluttered and overwhelming.
  • Few people knew what their AMC Stubs membership gave them until late in checkout.
  • People value ordering food ahead, but the menu is inconsistent and slow to browse.
  • People wanted reminders, reviews, and saved favorites.

Four problems to solve

  1. Cluttered informationToo many options at once, and a theatre list that jumped between locations.
  2. Hidden membership valueStubs benefits were not explained where people make decisions.
  3. Group planning outside the appNo way to coordinate seats or split the cost with friends.
  4. Nothing beyond bookingNo reason to open the app except to buy a ticket.

The redesign

We sketched the flows as low-fidelity wireframes first, linked them into a clickable prototype, then built the high-fidelity version in Figma in AMC's existing visual style.

  • Choose a theatre first, with favorites up top and only locations that have showtimes.
  • Membership details on the home screen and the movie page, with reward progress and a side-by-side comparison of tiers.
  • A reorganized food menu with combos and popular items first.
  • Split the bill: assign seats to friends and divide the cost evenly or by hand.
  • Share a showtime link to a group chat.
  • Follow a movie or theatre to get reminders, and rate and review films in the app.

Testing the prototype

87average SUS score, in the excellent range. Individual scores ran from 72.5 to 100
4 of 5said the redesigned flow reduced mental effort
5 of 5preferred the built-in split payment to using a separate app

Five people took part in moderated remote sessions of 25 to 30 minutes. Each worked through four scenarios in the Figma prototype: booking, membership, split the bill, and reminders. We recorded task success, error types, and completion time, and finished with the System Usability Scale.

“This eliminates having to use an external app to figure out payments.”

Usability test participant
  • Membership tiers were clear to all five once found, but finding them was harder. One participant tapped the profile icon first, expecting membership to be there.
  • Three of five expected their reminder list under their profile, not in Coming Soon. Two asked for calendar syncing or sharing.
  • Three preferred the custom split for fairness and two chose the equal split for speed. The custom split felt slow because items are assigned one at a time.

What I would change next

  • Shorten the custom split, and test letting each person add their own items.
  • Give clearer confirmation when a reminder is set, and explore calendar syncing and sharing.
  • Make membership easier to find, including from the profile.
