Deciding Under Pressure In progress

An experiment on whether the tone an AI agent uses changes the decisions people make when it asks for approval.

Role
Researcher. Study design, literature review, and survey
Timeline
2026 – present
Methods
Controlled experiment, literature review, survey
Design
2 × 3 factorial
Context
Human-AI Interaction, Pace University
Status
In progress. Findings will be added as the study completes

The question

AI agents increasingly ask people to approve an action before they take it. The way an agent phrases that request is a design choice, and it may change the outcome. This study asks a focused question: when an agent asks for approval, does the tone it uses change whether people say yes, and how quickly they decide?

Background

I started with a literature review across human-AI interaction, persuasive technology, and decision-making under time pressure. The review shapes the hypotheses and helps separate the effect of tone from the effect of urgency itself.

Study design

The experiment uses a 2 × 3 design: two levels of time pressure crossed with three agent tones. Participants work through approval decisions presented by an AI agent while the tone and the pressure vary between conditions.

  • Time pressure: a low-pressure condition and a high-pressure condition.
  • Agent tone: three variations in how the agent frames the same request.
  • Measures: the decision itself, how long it takes, and self-reported confidence and trust afterward.

Where it stands

The design, literature review, and survey instrument are complete, and the study is being run now. I will add the findings, analysis, and what they mean for designing AI approval flows once data collection wraps up.
